## Main question
Same model, same data, same walk-forward — does it matter whether a minibatch is 1024 shuffled ROWS or every stock of a few shuffled DAYS? The model is pinned: the from-scratch torch MLP with sklearn's exact initial weights that tracks mlp/e33_fenc_a64_32_16_identity_lre3_al100 (identity (64,32,16), alpha 100, lr 1e-3, fenccausal 44f, rolling 130/130, embargo 20).

## What was tried
8 arms × 10 seeds (42-51), all n=510 on one window (calmar comparable throughout). Gate: e104_row_s42 reproduces torch_scratch/e33repl_scratch_SKINIT_* bit for bit (max |Δcorr| over 510 periods = 0.0e+00). Arms: row (1024 rows, baseline), rowfix (1024 rows, L2 pinned to 1024), row900 (900 rows, L2 pinned), day3 (3 days ≈ 900 rows, L2 pinned), day3lit (3 days, L2 ÷ rows-in-batch), row300 (300 rows), day1 (1 day ≈ 300 rows), day1lit (1 day, L2 ÷ rows). Controls separate "batches are days" from "the penalty moved" and from "batches are smaller". Because the day batch has no fixed row count, the loss weights each DAY 1/k and the L2 term is pinned to alpha/1024. Then a 100-seed pass (E104_TAG=s100): row, row900, day3 extended to seeds 42-141 (270 more runs) for CORR/MMC/dd(pay) distributions.

## What was found
FINDINGS-BACKED. The paired result (per period, Newey-West lag 20, on the seed-mean payout delta; seed t treats each seed's mean delta as one observation):
- **What a batch is made of has no detectable effect.** day3 − row900 (composition only, same size, same steps) = +0.00007 payout, NW se 0.00074, t +0.09, seed t +0.07 — a tight null, interval ≈ [−0.0014, +0.0015]. At 300 rows both day1 and row300 are a constant predictor on every seed (day1 − row300 = 0 exactly).
- **Batch SIZE matters, through optimiser-step count.** Every arm decays toward the origin; a smaller batch takes more steps before the stop (≈4,100 vs ≈2,500) and shrinks the net linear map 10-40 orders of magnitude (|W| 1e-26..1e-55 at 300 rows vs 1e-15..1e-17 at 1024). row900 − rowfix (size only) = −0.00381 (t −7.03), row300 − rowfix = −0.04219 (t −7.85).
- **The L2 penalty must be pinned once batch size varies.** sklearn divides the L2 term by rows-in-batch, so a naive 900-row day batch runs a 14% stronger penalty; the literal port day3lit − row loses 0.01477 payout (t −6.85) where the pinned day3 costs nothing.
- **The seed picks the outcome, not the scheme.** row/rowfix/row900/day3 land in the same state on every seed (five work ≈0.13, seed 46 constant, seeds 47/49 sign-flipped). One seed misleads: on seed 42 alone day3 − row is +0.00824 payout (t +4.18), but plain row batches with only the L2 pin get +3.63 on that seed, and across 10 seeds the L2-pin gain is seed t +0.62.
- **100-seed pass.** day3 vs its size-matched twin row900 is the same distribution: 20 constants and 22 negatives each, 93/100 seeds in the same state; paired CORR −0.00148 (t −0.89), MMC −0.00001 (t −0.01), dd(pay) Wilcoxon p 0.059. day3 vs the baseline row is +0.00388 CORR (seed t +1.51), but row900 has the same lead, so it is batch size / the L2 pin, not days — and that pair's payout sign FLIPPED between 10 seeds (−0.0014) and 100 (+0.0018). The arm's median CORR over 100 seeds is 0.018 (seeds 42-51 alone: 0.113); only 30 of 100 reach CORR 0.10, 14 are constant, 25 negative, and the seed-42 row (CORR 0.1297, the one tracking the sklearn arm) is the 79th percentile of its own configuration.

Caveats recorded: this alpha-100 arm does not learn in the usual sense (the map shrinks to float noise), so the null is about a model decaying to zero and says little about day batches for one that fits; the question is still open for a non-degenerate model (lower alpha, or relu). Every day in all four training windows holds exactly 300 stocks, so ragged counts were exercised by the runner's self-test, not the data.
